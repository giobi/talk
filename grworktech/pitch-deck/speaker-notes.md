# GR Work Tech — Speaker Notes

## Slide 1: Title
Open with context: "We're here to walk through Brain Protocol — what it is, who it's for, and how we plan to bring it to market." Keep it short, Ide knows the background from Session 1.

## Slide 2: The Problem
The core insight: **AI has a memory problem, not a capability problem.** ChatGPT is brilliant but amnesiac — every session starts from zero. Meanwhile, companies spend years building expertise that lives in people's heads and nowhere else. When someone leaves, that knowledge is gone. Generic AI tools can answer questions but can't learn YOUR business. Pause on the highlight: "The problem isn't AI capability. It's AI memory."

## Slide 3: The Solution
Brain Protocol is not another AI chat tool. It's a **specification** — an open standard for building AI workspaces that remember. Four key points to hit:
- Structured knowledge base (not a folder dump — organized by memory type)
- WESP memory model mirrors human cognition (cite Tulving 1972, Baddeley & Hitch 1974 if asked)
- LLM-agnostic = no vendor lock-in. Switch from Claude to Gemini to whatever comes next. The brain stays.
- Git-versioned = auditable history, rollback, transparency. Critical for compliance.

## Slide 4: How It Works
Walk through each memory layer. Use Giobi's own brain as the live example if appropriate:
- **Wiki/** = the permanent record. People, companies, projects. Like a company Wikipedia that the AI reads.
- **Diary/** = what happened. Meeting notes, decisions, events. Chronological institutional memory.
- **Skills/** = how to do things. Automations, integrations, playbooks the AI can execute.
- **Working memory** = current context. What's happening right now.
- **Agentic layer** = THE differentiator. The AI doesn't just know — it DOES. Sends emails, produces deliverables, manages workflows. This is where the CEO value prop lives.

## Slide 5: Customer Segmentation
Key message: we segment by ROLE, not by industry. This is deliberate — avoids competing with our own clients.
- C-level feels the pain most: drowning in operational noise, can't focus on strategy
- Directors lose domain knowledge when teams churn
- Managers drown in process and compliance overhead
- Operators waste time on admin instead of value work

**Go-to-market is top-down.** Land the C-level first — they experience the value personally (agentic layer handles their operational overhead), then they push adoption down through the org.

## Slide 6: Market Landscape
Walk through the comparison matrix. Key talking points:
- **Dust.tt** ($6M ARR, VC-backed) — multi-model agents but no persistent memory, no custom deployment
- **Glean** ($200M ARR, $7.2B valuation) — enterprise search, completely wrong for SMBs, starts at $350K/year
- **Notion AI** — massive user base but agents are task-scoped, not company-brain. Credit-based pricing gets expensive
- **Lindy.ai** — good on agentic capability but no shared knowledge graph, each agent is independent
- **Personal.ai** — closest on memory but no agentic capabilities, more "chatbot with memory"

**The gap:** No one combines all six columns. That's our space.

## Slide 7: Competitive Advantage
Six cards, each is a moat:

2. **Agentic** — acts, not just answers
3. **Custom-fit** — every brain is tailored, not a shared SaaS
4. **Vendor-agnostic** — swap LLMs without migration

## Slide 8: Business Model
"AI Process Outsourcing" — deliberately positioned against traditional IT consulting.
- **Audit**: understand the company, map knowledge, identify opportunities. This is the entry point.
- **Deployment**: custom brain setup, integrations, training. This is the deliverable.
- **Retainer**: multi-year contract, continuous optimization. This is the revenue engine.

Target: 10-15 high-value contracts, not 10,000 SaaS users. The sartorial approach IS the product — not a bug, the model.

If asked about pricing: still calibrating, but think professional services pricing (EUR 5-15K/month retainer range), not SaaS pricing ($29/seat/month).
